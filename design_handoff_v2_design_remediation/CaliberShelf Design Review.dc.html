DESIGN & UI REVIEW · AUG 2026

CaliberShelf

A critical read of the interface as built — grounded in the source at fairorth/CaliberShelf@master, not screenshots. 26 findings, each with what I saw, why it matters, and what I'd change.

SCOPE · nav · collection · watch page · add/capture · photo panels · reports · theme tokens USER · single operator (you)
THE FIVE CALLS
01

The collection has no place to be looked at

Every route into a watch — table row, gallery tile, hero “View watch →” — lands on /watch/[id]/edit, a three-card form with ~30 inputs. For an app whose whole point is beautiful watches and beautiful photographs of them, the destination is a database record. Restore a view page as the default and make editing a deliberate mode.

02

The photography half of the system has no interface

The lab is real and rigorous — C1 preset, folder-per-watch routing, CV triage, coverage matrix, reshoot list — and it all lives in docs, mjs scripts and a local _photo-report.html. The app it feeds shows none of it, even though watch_image_scores is already in Postgres. This is the biggest unbuilt surface in the product, and the most differentiating.

03

Two primary colors are competing

globals.css says brass is “reserved for prices · never an interactive accent”, then brass drives the Save button, the Add CTA, required asterisks, card borders, dropzones and toggles — while steel-blue --primary drives the nav pills and the hero button. Two accents at equal weight means neither reads as “the action”.

04

Navigation hides the app from itself

Eleven destinations across four semantic groups sit behind a hamburger that stays a hamburger at 1920px, labelled with OS-dependent emoji, while a two-item segmented control takes the center of the bar. Deals, Reports, Guides, Straps, Brands and the Wear Log are invisible on a desktop until you open a menu. On a single-user desktop-first tool, that's the wrong trade.

05

The visual system is tuned, not defined

Type sizes are hand-nudged literals — 9.5, 10.5, 11, 12.5, 13.5, 14.5, 15.5, 19, 23, 34px — and radii bypass the token scale (13px, 11px, 10px, 7px alongside rounded-2xl). Individually each is a good judgement call; together there's no scale to design against, which is why new screens don't feel like old ones.

SECTION A

Information architecture

How the app is divided, and what those divisions imply about what matters.

A1 CRITICAL

Edit is the only watch page

watch/[id]/page.tsx redirects to /edit; the comment says the view-only page “was retired”. So the collection table, gallery tiles, hover thumbnails, the home hero CTA and Quick Capture's success state all deliver you into a form with a permanently docked save bar, a Delete button and an unsaved-changes dialog.

Two costs. Emotionally, the collection is never presented — it's only ever maintained; the photo work never gets a stage. Practically, every casual look risks an accidental edit, and the 420px photo column is squeezed next to a three-column field grid, so neither reads well.

Do: bring back /watch/[id] as a presentation page — large photo (contained, not cropped), a spec sheet in mono, wear + valuation + timegrapher as read-only strips, and a single “Edit” affordance. Keep the form intact behind it. If you liked the one-page speed, put field-level inline editing on the view page instead of a second route.

A2 HIGH

A hamburger at every breakpoint

The header runs three navigation systems at once: a centered Home/Collection segmented control (desktop-only), an always-available hamburger holding eleven links in four unlabelled groups, and a persistent Add Watch button. Opening the menu expands the header and pushes the page down rather than overlaying it, so navigating costs a layout jump.

The four groups are meaningful — assets · analysis · acquisition & imagery · system — but the user sees only separator lines and emoji, so the grouping teaches nothing.

Do: on ≥lg, a 200px left rail with those four groups given visible headings, current item marked, collapsing to icon-only at md and to the drawer at sm. Then delete the segmented control — Home and Collection are just the first two rail items — and let the header hold identity, global search, Add, theme, account. Bonus: the rail gives Reports and Deals a permanent home, which is where the agent work actually surfaces.

A3 HIGH

Four ways to add a photo, three interaction models

Add-watch has a drag/paste dropzone with client-side downscaling. The edit page has a Photo Uploader with a device-dependent button pair and a global paste listener. /capture is a three-step wizard — and appears in no nav group, so it's reachable only by typing the URL. Batch Import is a fourth path. Only the add flow downscales before upload, so the other paths are the ones that hit the “photo may be too large” error.

Do: one upload component (dropzone + paste + camera + downscale) used in all four places, and decide Capture's fate: either promote it into the nav as the studio-session entry point (see D1) or delete the route. An orphaned route is worse than no route.

A4 MEDIUM

“Gallery” names three different things

The route /dashboard is labelled “Home” in the nav but carries the metadata title “Gallery”. /gallery is the inspiration-image board. And Collection's tile view is called Gallery. Three referents, one word — and the browser tab is the one that lies.

Do: Home (the stage) · Collection → Table/Tiles · Inspiration (rename /gallery, it isn't your watches). Fix the /dashboard title while you're there. Same pass: /add vs /collection/new are two live add routes.

A5 LOW

The Reports index has a dead card

Nine cards in a uniform grid; Wear Summary carries a “Coming soon” pill but is still a full-size clickable card leading to a placeholder. Eight equal cards also give no sense of which reports you actually read weekly.

Do: make the unbuilt card non-interactive, and rank the grid — Collection Summary and Attention Needed are the daily ones; Agent Execution Review is the one with a live cost number, so give it a value on the card (last run, spend this month) rather than only prose.

SECTION B

The collection page

Your most-used screen, and the one carrying the most accumulated controls.

B1 HIGH

Nine controls in one wrapping row

Title, search, category select, Filters dialog, sort select, direction toggle, the count/total/value/gain string, the tile-size slider and the view switcher all share a single flex-wrap line. At laptop widths the wrap point is unpredictable, and the ml-auto group jumps between rows as filters change.

The status string is doing too much: 12 of 34 · $48,200 · value $52,100 +8.1% is four different facts in one run of small type, two of them only appearing in one filter mode.

Do: two bands. Line 1: “Collection” + a small stat strip (count / cost / value / delta as labelled numbers in mono). Line 2: search grows, then category, Filters, sort, and the view switcher pinned right; move the tile-size slider into the tile view's own corner or drop it for three fixed densities. That also gets rid of the hand-rolled SELECT_CLASS native select sitting next to shadcn Selects.

B2 CRITICAL

Filters persist invisibly

Filters and sort are saved to localStorage and restored on mount, but the page shows no summary of what's active — only the dialog knows. Come back a week later with brand + tier + “tracked only” still set and the collection is quietly wrong; the “12 of 34” is the sole hint, and it's easy to read as “34 watches, showing a page”.

The status/wish-list toggles compound it: they change the denominator too, so filtered state can look like a complete collection.

Do: a chip row under the toolbar — one removable chip per active filter, plus “Clear all”, hidden entirely when nothing is set. Consider not persisting filters across sessions at all (persist sort and view; reset filters), and put the category filter in the same chip row rather than in a second control.

B3 HIGH

Two sort systems, arbitrated by deleting a storage key

The toolbar dropdown sorts, and the table headers sort independently; the view clears TABLE_SORT_KEY so a dropdown choice wins next mount. The user's model breaks: the dropdown can read “Sort: Brand” while the table is sorted by price, and neither control shows the other's state.

Do: one sort state owned by the view. In table view, headers are the only control (drop the dropdown; keep the header arrow as truth). In tile view, keep the dropdown. Same state object, so switching views preserves order.

B4 HIGH

Zebra striping in the accent hue

Rows alternate a neutral wash with oklch(0.6 0.11 233 / 0.12) — the same family as --primary. Tinted rows read as selected or flagged, and half your table is permanently “on”. There's also no hover row treatment, so pointing at a row gives no feedback while eleven columns of muted text ask you to track across.

Do: drop the blue band. Either a single 3–4% white stripe or no stripe plus a 1px hairline, and spend the contrast budget on a clear hover (row background lift + brass left edge) and a real selected state you can then use for bulk actions.

B5 MEDIUM

Identical-looking cells, three destinations

In one row: Category navigates to a filtered URL, Brand calls a filter callback, Model and Nickname open the edit page, and the Photo opens the edit page too. All four are underline-on-hover text. Nothing distinguishes “filter by this” from “open this”, and there's no whole-row target, so the largest click area in the row does nothing.

Do: the row is the watch — clicking anywhere opens it. Move “show all of this brand/category” to a small filter glyph revealed on cell hover (or a right-click/context menu), and mark those cells with a consistent affordance. Also: eleven columns is a lot of “—”; make the column set choosable and default to eight.

B6 MEDIUM

Tiles crop the subject

Tile covers are square object-cover with a hover scale — on a portrait watch shot that cuts the lugs and strap, the parts that carry the model's character. The tile header also reserves a 28px strip above every photo for a movement-type chip, so the photo is pushed down and the chip carries more visual weight than the watch.

Do: you already store dial_focal_x/y/zoom — use it for tile framing, not just the hero. Let the photo start at the tile's top edge and move the type chip into the metadata footer with the caliber, where it belongs. A 4:5 tile suits wrist shots better than 1:1.

SECTION C

The watch form

1,300 lines and, today, the whole watch experience. Mostly well built — three specific problems.

C1 CRITICAL

Unsaved work escapes through every exit but one

The dirty flag guards exactly one door — the form's own Return button. The nav header, the hamburger links, the logo, the browser back button and a page reload all leave silently, and there's no beforeunload handler. On the page where you enter 30 fields plus agent autofill results, that's the highest-stakes loss in the app.

Second-order: dirty is one-way. onInput → true, never back to false, so typing a character and deleting it leaves “Unsaved changes” glowing brass forever — which trains you to ignore the indicator that the guard depends on.

Do: add a beforeunload guard while dirty, and compare against initial values so dirty can clear. Better still, given single-user desktop use: autosave per field on blur with a “Saved 14:32” stamp in the bar, and keep Delete behind the dialog. Then the bar stops being a gate and becomes a status line.

C2 HIGH

The form is dark-only

Every field uses FIELD = "bg-[#1b212a] border-white/12 …", and date inputs force [color-scheme:dark]. In light mode you get near-black fields with white-alpha borders on a pale page. The same literal-over-token pattern appears in the nav pill (bg-white/[0.04]), tile chips, tile backgrounds (#222a33 → #12161c) and the add dropzone. You ship a theme toggle in the header; half the app doesn't honour it.

Do: decide honestly whether light mode is a feature. If yes, replace every hex/white-alpha literal with a token (--input, --card, --muted) and drop the forced color-scheme; if no, remove the toggle and the light block — a dark-only tool for a jewellery-photography app is a defensible position, and it's cheaper than maintaining two truths.

C3 MEDIUM

Three-column field grid, no rhythm

Card 1 puts Brand, Model, Nickname, Reference, Serial, Purchase Date, Price and Box into a uniform three-column grid, so a 20-character serial gets the same width as a 4-character price and the eye finds no anchor. The three checkbox rows below each span all columns with the description as a separate sibling span, giving three near-identical full-width lines of small type. The Specifications card then measures nine numeric fields at equal width where they'd read far better as a compact measurement block with units aligned.

Do: size fields to their content (a 5-char mono input for mm values, full width for serial/notes), group the case measurements into a labelled 2×4 spec block with right-aligned mono numbers and a unit suffix inside the field, and turn the three status checkboxes into one “Status” control — Owned / Coming soon / Wish list are mutually exclusive in your own filter logic, so a segmented control matches the data model better than three checkboxes.

SECTION D

Photography

The half of the system with the most craft behind it and the least interface in front of it.

D1 CRITICAL

There is no Photo Lab in the app

The lab exists as documentation and terminal output: a C1 recipe, overhead geometry, focus-stacking parameters, per-watch capture folders created by sync-watch-folders, and photo-score.mjs producing sharpness/dup triage, shot-card grades, a coverage matrix and a reshoot list into a local HTML file. In the app, photography is one uploader button and a grid of undifferentiated squares. The scores table is already migrated; nothing renders it.

This is the gap between what the system is and what the UI admits it is. It's also where the app stops being another collection tracker.

Do: add Photo Lab as a top-level destination with three views. Coverage — a watch × angle matrix (flat dial-on, hero three-quarter, profile, caseback, macro) with cells scored/empty, so “what still needs shooting” is a glance instead of a script. Session — pick the watch, see its folder, C1 checklist and last frames; the shoot is the unit of work, not the upload. Review — the scored frames from a run, largest-first, keyboard-driven accept/reject with “set as cover” and angle tagging in one pass. That report is already generated; it just deserves to live where the collection lives.

D2 HIGH

The photo panel fights the photographer

Four specific frictions in photo-gallery.tsx: the lightbox opens on onDoubleClick (undiscoverable, and unreliable on touch — the visible Zoom button only exists in the single-photo case); actions live in a select-then-act footer whose state is announced as “Selected: Photo 3”; the hero tile is index 0, not the cover, so your chosen cover can sit as a small tile with a badge while another photo takes the big slot; and there's no reordering, no captions, no angle labels. Every image is also object-cover in a square, so the review view crops the frame you carefully composed with margin for straightening.

Do: single click opens the lightbox; do the work there (arrow keys between frames, C to set cover, X to delete, 1–5 to tag angle) since that's the only place the pixels are big enough to judge. In the panel, hero = cover, hover reveals per-tile actions, drag reorders, and detail images are object-contain on a neutral field. Reserve cover-cropping for dense grids only.

D3 HIGH

Quick Capture has the flow inverted

Capture → select watch → confirm, one frame per pass, with the watch chosen from a grid of the entire collection after the shot. A real session is the opposite shape: one watch, many frames, over minutes. Three taps of overhead per frame, a 128px emoji shutter, and a text step indicator make it feel like a form rather than a camera.

Do: invert it — choose the watch once (or inherit the active session watch from D1), then shoot repeatedly with an instant filmstrip of what you just took and undo per frame. Accept multi-select from the gallery in one go. Match the lab's folder-per-watch mental model so app captures and tethered captures land in the same place conceptually.

D4 MEDIUM

The hero is a screensaver you can't operate

The Living Dial is the best-crafted thing in the app — real case, lugs, crown, a brass rim line phase-locked to the wall clock. But it auto-advances on a timer with no pause, no previous/next, no keyboard, and the caption's only action is “View watch →”, which opens the edit form (see A1). prefers-reduced-motion is honoured for the sunburst dial only — not for the swap or the 60-second sweep. And the whole thing sits behind a Living Dial / Display Box toggle whose state is invisible until you look.

Do: hover pauses, ← → steps, click the dial goes to the watch. Extend the reduced-motion query to freeze the rotation. Then let the hero earn its screen space: one line of context that isn't in the caption today — days since last worn, latest valuation delta, or “no hero-angle photo yet”, which makes the stage feed the lab.

SECTION E

Visual system

The material story — navy, steel, brass, engraved serif — is genuinely good. It's the enforcement that's missing.

E1 CRITICAL

Brass broke its own rule

The token is annotated “reserved for prices (never an interactive accent)”. In practice brass is: the Save button, the Add-watch primary CTA, required asterisks, every spec card's left border and header wash, the “NEW ENTRY” eyebrow, the Living Dial active pill, dropzone iconography, column-resize hover, autofill highlight rings — and prices. Meanwhile steel-blue --primary owns the nav pills, the hero CTA, focus rings and the photo-selection ring.

Consequence: on the watch page the brass Save button and the blue selection ring compete for “most important thing”, and because brass is also every card's border, the button doesn't read as special at all.

Do: pick one and write it down. My recommendation, since brass is your brand: brass = action + brand (primary buttons, active nav, focus) and steel-blue = data (charts, links, informational chips), with value/price in a neutral high-contrast mono instead of a colour. Then strip brass from decoration — card borders and header washes go to a neutral hairline — so the one brass thing on screen is the thing to click.

E2 HIGH

Emoji as the icon system

Nav items are 📋🏷️〰️📅📊🧭💰📸📦⚙️ℹ️; card titles are 🏷️⚙️📂 in brass chips; subsection eyebrows are ⏱️🔩✨; empty states are ⌚; Capture's shutter is 📷; success is ✅. lucide-react is already installed and used for the view switcher, theme toggle and zoom. So the app runs two icon languages, one of which is rendered by the OS — colour, weight and metrics you don't control, glyphs that fight a navy-and-brass palette, and screen readers announcing “clipboard” for Collection.

Do: lucide everywhere at one size (16px nav / 18px headers), currentColor, aria-hidden. Keep ✨ for the AI autofill button only — there it's a deliberate convention, not decoration — and replace the ⌚ empty states with a quiet outline mark or your own logo mark, which you already have as a component.

E3 HIGH

No type scale, and page titles disagree

Sizes are set as one-off literals: 9.5, 10.5, 11, 12, 12.5, 13, 13.5, 14.5, 15.5, 19, 20, 23, 34px. Page h1s range from text-lg (Collection, Config, Reports) to text-2xl (watch) to 34px (Add) — so the Add form shouts and the collection whispers, with no hierarchy logic behind it. Fraunces also appears at 15.5px semibold inside table rows, where a display serif adds texture to the densest part of the UI.

Do: six steps and stop — say 11 / 13 / 15 / 19 / 26 / 38 — as CSS vars, with mono locked to 12/13 and Fraunces reserved for ≥19px. One page-title size everywhere. Brand names in table cells go to the sans at 14–15px medium; the serif then means “heading”, consistently.

E4 MEDIUM

Six radii, none from the token scale

You define --radius with a derived sm→4xl scale, then hardcode 13px tiles, 11px nav, 10px buttons, 7px logo mark alongside rounded-2xl and rounded-lg. Nobody notices one; everyone feels the aggregate as slight sloppiness.

Do: three radii — control 8px, card 14px, pill full — and delete the rest. A watch app can carry a slightly tighter radius than the shadcn default; it reads more instrument-like.

E5 LOW

Muted text is doing 80% of the talking

In the table, gallery footer and hero caption, nearly everything is text-muted-foreground (#8c95a0) — often at 10.5–13px, sometimes at /70 or /80 opacity on top. When everything is secondary, nothing is primary, and the smallest sizes fall below AA contrast on card surfaces.

Do: per surface, name one primary value (brand+model in a row; brand in a tile) at full foreground and let the rest be muted — but stop stacking opacity modifiers on an already-muted token, and floor small text at 12px.

SECTION F

Accessibility & polish

Single-user today — but two of these will bite you personally, not hypothetically.

F1 HIGH

Pinch-zoom is disabled on the phone

maximumScale: 1 in the root viewport blocks zoom app-wide. On a photography PWA that's self-defeating — checking dial sharpness on a phone is exactly a pinch-zoom task — and it's a WCAG 1.4.4 failure.

Do: remove maximumScale (keep viewportFit: cover), and give the lightbox real pinch/double-tap zoom to 100%.

F2 MEDIUM

Semantics missing where you'd feel it

Sortable headers use buttons with ▲▼⇅ glyphs but no aria-sort; the hamburger has no aria-expanded; nav emoji aren't hidden from the accessibility tree; column resize handles are pointer-only (no keyboard path). The $$ price-check marker and ◷ wear count carry meaning only in a title attribute — invisible on touch, and cryptic anywhere.

Do: a half-day sweep: aria-sort on headers, aria-expanded/aria-controls on the menu, aria-hidden on decorative glyphs, and replace $$/◷ with lucide icons plus visible labels in a legend row.

F3 LOW

Two buttons and a paragraph explaining them

The Add flow ends with “Save & add details →” (brass) and “Save & close”, followed by a sentence explaining what each does — and the explanation contradicts itself slightly (“returns to the watch”). If two buttons need a paragraph, the labels aren't carrying their weight.

Do: one primary Save watch that lands on the watch page (which, per A1, is where you'd continue anyway), plus a quiet “Save and add another” for batch entry. No explanatory paragraph.

WORTH KEEPING

Criticism reads louder than praise, so to be explicit about what's already strong: the material concept — navy-black surfaces, steel-blue interaction, brass for value, an engraved serif — is a real point of view, and rare. The Living Dial is a genuinely delightful piece of craft. Agent transparency is exemplary: cost per call shown inline, sources linked, empty-fields-only fills, unverified references flagged amber until a human confirms — most products hide all four.

The taxonomy discipline (category vs complications vs user-configured tiers) is cleaner than most commercial collection tools, resizable persisted columns and paste-to-upload are the marks of someone building for their own real use, and the sticky save bar with a dirty indicator is the right pattern — it just needs to be trustworthy. None of the findings above are about the app being badly built. They're about a strong system that hasn't yet been written down and enforced, and about a photography practice that deserves a front end.
