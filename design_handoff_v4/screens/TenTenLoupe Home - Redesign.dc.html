TURN 5 · COMPOSING A FLAT SHOT FOR A 3:2 STAGE

Give the dial somewhere to sit

A dial is round, so the instinct on a flat shot is to centre it and crop tight — which produces a square, and a square costs you half the stage. The fix is not to shoot the dial smaller; it is to shoot wider, and put the dial somewhere other than the middle. Your R10's native 3:2 is exactly the stage aspect, so an uncropped horizontal frame fills the page edge to edge.

FIVE THINGS TO DO AT THE CAMERA
Shoot landscape, deliver 3:2. Turn the camera horizontal and do not crop. The R10's native frame already matches the stage exactly. Dial on a third line, not the centre. Centre it where a vertical third crosses a horizontal one — about two-thirds across and one-third down, or its mirror. Let the strap enter from a corner. A diagonal gives the empty half something to do and stops it reading as a gap. Light the negative space, don't just leave it dark. A gradient falling off across the empty side is what the bloom picks up. Leave the dial some margin. Roughly 40% of the frame height, not 90% — the bloom then reads as a rim of light rather than a field, and the watch still dominates because the frame is wide, not because the dial is huge.
TURN 4 · THE FRAME SHOULD FIT THE PHOTOGRAPH

Stop forcing 3:2

The Addiesdive frame is 0.81:1 — portrait. In a fixed 3:2 box it gets 54% of the width and the bloom takes the other 46%, so the loudest thing on the page is a blur. The widest-frame preference helps when a watch has options, but this watch has exactly one photograph, so no selection rule can save it. The container itself is the problem: a fixed aspect box means every photo that isn't 3:2 pays for the difference in bloom.

Fix: fix the height, let the width follow the photograph. The bloom stops being a field the photo floats in and becomes a spill of light around its edges — the same colour, doing the same job, at a tenth of the area. A wide reclining shot fills the content width; a square dial-on shot sits centred and compact. Neither is cropped, and neither is drowned.

THE RULE

Stage height stays fixed at 470px. Width = 470 × aspect, capped at the content width — so a 3:2 or wider frame still fills the page edge to edge and nothing about the current best case changes. The bloom becomes a soft radial spill extending ~40px past the photo at low opacity, which also retires the scrim entirely: there is no large dark field left to correct, so the luminance-adaptive mapping from Phase 8 §2 becomes a small opacity adjustment rather than a fix. Aspect comes from the stored image dimensions, so the box is correct on first paint with no layout shift.

TURN 3 · HOME REDESIGN · BOXES · DISPLAY CASE

One spine, not two columns

The alignment problem you spotted is structural, not a padding bug: two independent columns of different heights can only agree at the top, so the left always ends early and leaves a hole. The fix is to stop having two columns. Everything now stacks down one centre line in the order you actually read it — photograph → who it is → the strip → the facts — which also kills the duplicate-image problem, because the strip is the only place thumbnails live. ON YOUR POINT 4 — IT ISN'T A BUG

The full-screen zoomed watch that reverts the moment you move the pointer is glance mode, working exactly as designed and shipped. I checked the code: it is wired, with a Config toggle and an idle-delay list. It feels random because your dwell is 15 seconds and your glance delay is 60 — so the watch changes four times, then the screen transforms, and the two events look unrelated. Nothing is broken; it is undiscoverable, which is my fault in the spec. Three fixes: raise the dwell to 90s so the two timers read as one rhythm, make the MOVE TO RETURN pill unmissable (check whether it is even rendering — you didn't mention seeing it), and add a one-time toast the first few times it engages: “Glance mode — move the mouse to return. Configurable in Config.”
