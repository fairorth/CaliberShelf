# Shooting notes — composing for the Light Table

**Not an implementation doc.** Nothing here is for Claude Code; this is for the
first EOS Utility session. It exists because the home page's stage geometry
(Phase 8 §2.1) makes the *shape* of a frame matter as much as its content.

Worked example with diagrams: section `5a` of
`screens/TenTenLoupe Home - Redesign.dc.html`.

## The rule that drives all of this

The home stage has a **fixed height and a width that follows the photograph's
aspect**, capped at the content width. So:

- A **3:2 horizontal** frame fills the stage edge to edge. Best case.
- A **square or portrait** frame renders compact and centred, with a bloom of
  its own colour either side. Fine, but smaller.

The Canon R10 shoots 3:2 natively — the exact stage aspect. **An uncropped
horizontal frame is the best case, and it is also the default case.** Nothing
special is required to hit it except not cropping.

## Five things to do at the camera

1. **Shoot landscape, deliver 3:2.** Turn the camera horizontal and do not crop.
2. **Dial on a third line, not the centre.** Centre it where a vertical third
   crosses a horizontal one — about two-thirds across and one-third down, or its
   mirror.
3. **Let the strap enter from a corner.** A diagonal gives the empty half
   something to do and stops it reading as a gap.
4. **Light the negative space; don't just leave it dark.** A gradient falling off
   across the empty side is what the bloom picks up and spreads onto the page.
5. **Leave the dial margin — roughly 40% of frame height, not 90%.** This is the
   counter-intuitive one. When a dial looks small the instinct is to move closer,
   but that produces a square, and a square costs half the stage. The watch
   dominates because the *frame* is wide, not because the dial is big. A dial at
   40% of a full-width 3:2 frame occupies far more screen than one at 90% of a
   portrait crop.

Shoot the mirror composition too (dial left, strap in from bottom-right) so the
rotation doesn't always lean the same way. Free variety, decided at the camera.

## By angle class

The five classes are not equally suited to the stage, and that is fine — they
have different jobs.

| Angle | Shape | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `hero` | 3:2 horizontal | The stage's best case. This is the frame the rotation lands on, so it is the one worth composing hardest. |
| `profile` | 3:2 horizontal | Watch reclining on its side, strap sweeping across. Fills the frame naturally — often the most striking on the home page. |
| `flat` | wants to be square | A dial is round, so a tight flat shot is square by default. Compose it horizontally with the dial off-centre if you want flat shots to hold the stage. |
| `caseback` | wants to be square | Same as flat. Usually fine compact. |
| `macro` | crop as the detail demands | A tight 1:1 crown crop *should* render small and precise. Don't fight it. |

## One decision worth making before the first frame

No frames have been shot through EOS Utility yet, so there is a one-time
opportunity: **pick one house background and one light setup and keep to it.**

The luminance-adaptive bloom copes with variety, but coping is not the goal.
A consistent house style makes the bloom *predictable* — the page recolours
gently as the rotation moves rather than lurching between a bright frame and a
dark one. That predictability is what makes an all-day screen feel composed
rather than lucky.

Legacy phone photos in the existing library will stay inconsistent, and the
aspect-fit rule handles them honestly. This is about everything shot from here.
