TURN 1 · HOME PAGE · THREE DIRECTIONS

Retiring the living dial

The circle is the problem. watch-hero.tsx crops each cover twice — square-to-circle mask, then focal point and zoom on top — inside a circle inset 4.82% of a 560px case. That is the awkward window, and no focal point fixes a composition the circle was never going to hold. All three directions keep the rotation and the key information, drop the mask, and carry the photography idea in the presentation. Refer to them as 1a, 1b, 1c.

Each is drawn in the real desktop shell: the 200px rail from nav-rail.tsx carrying the horizontal lockup and the five groups from nav-items.ts, and the 56px header with no brand (it is md:hidden — the rail owns the mark from md up) and no search box (hidden on /dashboard). So the stage each concept actually gets is 920px wide, not 1120.

1a

The Gallery Print

one photograph, matted and hung

The most photograph-respecting option, and the one least bothered by the rail — a centred print is indifferent to how wide the stage is. The frame adapts to the image instead of the image fighting the frame: portrait, square and landscape all sit in the same mat. The museum label carries specs on the left and capture data on the right, so every visit says “this is a photography practice.”

1b

The Light Table

a contact sheet you inspect with a loupe

The literal answer to the brand mark: the home page is the light table where frames get examined. One frame enlarged, the rest of the sheet beside it — hovering a thumbnail swaps the big frame, hovering the big frame raises a loupe that magnifies at 100%. The only concept that shows many photographs at once, which makes the loudest photography statement and doubles as the route into the Photo Lab. It is also the concept the rail costs most: at 920px the sheet drops to three columns.

1c

The Editorial Spread

cover story, one watch at a time

The boldest of the three and the one that best rewards a genuinely great photograph. Framing it against the real shell changed it: the rail owns the left edge, so the photo now bleeds right, to the top, right and bottom of the content area — which reads better anyway, since the type column lands beside the nav instead of across the page from it. The angle rail beneath the type shows what you actually shot, straight from the coverage set. No crop decision, because the layout bends to the photo: portrait takes the right half as shown, landscape runs full width across the top with the type beneath.

WHAT CARRIES OVER, AND WHAT THE DIAL BECOMES

Everything in watch-hero.tsx except the mask is worth keeping, and all three inherit it: the seeded shuffle, the per-device dwell preference, hover-to-pause, arrow-key stepping, the reduced-motion freeze, and the wear line. The dial motif survives as chrome in 1a and 1c — the brass rim line already laps once a minute as a live seconds hand, and it keeps exactly that job as the rotation timer, with no photograph behind it. dial_focal_x/y and dial_zoom then serve only the square gallery thumbnails in gallery-grid.tsx, where a centre-weighted crop of a composed frame is usually fine — so the framing editor can shrink to picking which frame is the hero, which the Photo Lab already does.
