TURN 2 · LIGHT TABLE · FLAIR

Warmth, without whimsy

Your screenshot diagnoses itself: a warm, coloured photograph sitting in a cold grey void. I lifted the Citizen frame out of it and sampled — dial #646f6e, surround #605243 — so every option below is judged against your real frame. The dryness is not the data. It is four fixable things: the letterbox is dead, the right column is empty whenever a watch has one frame, the nulls read as failures (0 / 5 angles, not yet worn, —), and the watch's own name is set smaller than the page title. Refer to these as 2a, 2b, 2c. They compose — 2a is a base layer, 2b a skin, 2c a mode.

Each is drawn at 920px, the real content width beside the 200px rail.

2a

Developed in colour

the frame lights its own room

The single highest-return change, and the one I would ship regardless of the others. The letterbox stops being grey: it fills with a heavily blurred, darkened copy of the frame itself, so a teal dial gives you a teal room and a warm desk shot gives you a warm one. Because the colour is derived from the photograph, the page recolours itself all day as the rotation moves, which is the “dancing by” feeling — motion of light, not motion of interface. The frame also drifts: a 1.00→1.07 scale across the full dwell, far too slow to read as animation, and a cross-fade between watches. The watch's name becomes the headline at 38px, and the frame is set larger.

The guardrail that keeps this from becoming slop: the derived colour is allowed to touch the bloom field and nothing else. Never text, never a border, never a control. Brass stays the only accent — otherwise this reintroduces the blue-accent creep the FIXES rounds cleaned up.

2b

The film edge

the light table, taken literally

The flair comes from the craft vocabulary instead of from colour: sprocket edges, rebate markings along the frame, a stock line, hand-numbered frames. It is the warmest of the three because the ground moves from cool slate to bone paper, and it earns its technical text — TENTENLOUPE 400 · 11/60 along the edge reads as film marking rather than as a data field. The contact sheet becomes an actual strip, and unshot angles are visible empty cells in the strip, which is the same fix as 2a's shot list but with more character. Best paired with 2a's bloom behind the frame; shown here on its own so you can judge the vocabulary.

2c

Glance mode

the page you actually leave up all day

This is the direct answer to “I intend to leave it up all day.” A working screen and an ambient screen want opposite things, and right now one layout is trying to be both — which is exactly why it reads as dry. So: after 60 seconds without input, the chrome fades away. The frame grows to fill the stage, the bloom carries the colour, and all that remains is the brand, the reference, one quiet line, and the brass ring keeping time. Move the mouse and the working view returns instantly. Nothing is lost and nothing is hidden — it is the same rotation, dressed for glancing at from across the room.

Cheap to build on what exists: one idle timer, one CSS class, no new data. It also inherits the reduced-motion rule — if motion is reduced, glance mode still fades in but the frame does not drift.

WHAT I WOULD DO

Ship 2a and 2c together and hold 2b. 2a fixes the actual defect — a photograph in a grey void — and costs one blurred copy of an image you already load. 2c gives you the all-day artifact you asked for, and is an idle timer plus a class. 2b is the most characterful and the most opinionated: film vocabulary is lovely until the day you shoot a watch that has nothing to do with film, and it also warms the whole ground, which is a bigger design-system decision than the other two. Try it after the first two are in.

Three smaller notes that apply to all of them. The nulls should read as invitations — never — give it a day instead of not yet worn, and the empty coverage cells as a shot list instead of 0 / 5 angles. The watch, not the page, should carry the largest type. And the dwell wants to be much longer than 30 seconds for an all-day screen — 90 to 120 makes it feel like a gallery rather than a slideshow, and it is already a preference you can change.
