# Summary

## WRONG — authoritative contradiction found (6)

1. **P15 Aronde 1954 — "2011 limited edition" / "rose-gold limited edition."** The rose gold **81018/000R-9657 is not a limited edition**; it was launched at SIHH 2011 as unlimited production. The 20-piece LE is the **white gold 81018/000G-9559 (2010, Japan only)**. The error appears twice on the card and could justify an unwarranted premium. *(WatchProSite Aronde background; MasterHorologer: "although not a limited/numbered edition")*

2. **P4 "1972 / Prestige de la France" — dating ref 37010 to the 1970s.** Ref **37010 is the 1997 re-edition**, not a 1970s watch. The 1970s originals are **35202 / 2091** (21 × 46mm, cal 1050/3), 35203 dual-time, 15207/15706 ladies. *(WatchProSite reference overview; Watches of Knightsbridge dates its 37010 "circa 2000")*

3. **P4 caliber — "manual-wind ultra-thin family" for 37010.** Ref 37010 uses **cal. 1055** (2.1mm). The 1972 original uses **cal. 1050/3**. Different movements, different watches.

4. **P6 Malte Power Reserve/Date 83060 — "1990s-2000s."** The 83060 (000G-9287 WG, 000R-9288 RG) was **introduced in 2007** per La Cote des Montres' dated press announcement. Correct: **c.2007–early 2010s**. No 1990s 83060 exists.

5. **P11 AMVOX2 — name and reference do not match.** **192.T.25 is the AMVOX2 *DBS* (Transponder)**, cal **751E**, 44mm. The **AMVOX2 Chronograph is 192.T4.40 / Q192T440** (43.7mm, cal **751B**, LE 750). Also, the Q-form actually paired with 192.T.25 in auction records is **Q192T450**, not "Q192T25". *(Sotheby's; Quill & Pad AMVOX overview; PrestigeTime)*

6. **P4 variant — "white or yellow gold depending specimen."** Every ref 37010 found is **white gold**; the yellow-gold Prestige de la France examples on the market are the **1970s original at 46 × 21mm**. Following this note leads to a different reference and era than the card intends.

## PARTIAL — roughly right, needs precision (13)

- **P1 caliber "V485 family."** "V485" is a real VC designation, but Beyond the Dial assigns **V485/P485 to the moonphase triple calendar** and **V495/P495 to the non-moonphase**; ref 4240 is the non-moonphase (4240L is the 1948 moonphase revision). A Phillips 1942 ref-4240 lot lists **cal. 455**. Verify caliber per example against the Extract rather than asserting it on the card.
- **P2 dates "1951-mid 1950s."** E501 with cal 497/497-1 ran **1951–1957**.
- **P3 dates "late 1950s-1960s."** E855 ran **1960 to c.1974**. Cal 825 dates from 1959. Also unstated: cal 825 is a **bumper** automatic.
- **P5 variant "yellow-gold Europe/Africa/Asia map."** Correct as the typical YG config, but **yellow gold is not exclusively that map** — Sotheby's has sold a YG "America" (43050/000J-17). Specify the map. The enamel is **champlevé**, not cloisonné (the brief says cloisonné), and cheap examples used **acid etching**. The 1994 launch series is a separate ref, **11992** (50 pcs).
- **P7 caliber — card names none.** It is **cal. 1206 RDT**, self-winding, F. Piguet 1150 base, 65h, 31 jewels. On an active negotiation this is the most checkable single spec.
- **P7 dimensions "~38 mm class."** Specialist spec sheets give **39mm**; some dealers say 38mm. Measure the watch.
- **P9 dimensions "35.7 x 43.1 mm."** Width holds; the 43.1 length is **not reproducible** — PrestigeTime and Shreve give **35.7 × 41mm × 12.7mm**, Sotheby's 43 × 34.5mm, WatchProSite 35 × 43mm. The card also omits the **~12.7–13.6mm thickness**, and omits that the calendar includes a **moonphase**.
- **P10 — the confusable reference is unnamed.** Q3028420/240.8.18 is one watch (all the "GMT" vs "GMT Duo Face" names refer to it). The trap is **Q3038420 / 240.8.72**, a different **automatic** Reverso Grande GMT/Date — the likely cause of the bimodal asks. Add a "not to be confused with" line. Card also omits thickness (~12–12.5mm).
- **P11 dates "c. 2006-2012."** AMVOX2 launched 2006, produced **c.2007–2011**; DBS from 2008.
- **P11 variant "titanium/PVD limited version."** JLC's executions are black-coated steel + titanium (250), **titanium (750)**, titanium + platinum (200). Edition size for 192.T.25 is quoted as **750 by dealers, 999 by Sotheby's** — confirm on the caseback.
- **P14 dates "late 2000s-2010s."** Launched **2010** (55th anniversary of cal 1003). Card should also print the **4.13mm** total thickness — it is the whole argument.
- **P16 dates "c. 2012-2024."** Right for this reference (39mm from 2012, replaced Jan 2024) but the Réserve de Marche line begins **2011 at 37mm**.
- **P18 caliber "5100."** It is **cal. 5100/1** — cal 5100 with a **tungsten rotor in place of the gold one**, which is exactly the steel model's positioning story. 60h, Geneva Seal.

## UNVERIFIED — could not establish either way (6)

- **P17 "c. 2019" end of production.** No authoritative discontinuation date for the Geophysic True Second. Searched JLC site, WatchBase, aBlogtoWatch, dealer listings.
- **P17 "tied to JLC's chronometric Geophysic lineage."** Widely repeated, but no page reached this session documents the 1958 Geophysic → 2015 True Second lineage; sources describe the 2015 watch only.
- **P18 "layered security-print-inspired dial, unusual case construction."** Sources reached (SJX, Monochrome, Time and Watches, WatchBase) describe the transparency and layering but do not document the security-printing provenance or the case-construction claim.
- **P18 "c. 2019" end of production** for the steel Quai de l'Île. 2016 start confirmed; no end year found.
- **P19 "c. 2022" end of production** for the Triple Calendrier 1942. 2017 launch confirmed; no discontinuation year found on VC's site, Monochrome or Forbes' launch coverage.
- **P21 "$17,000" pre-order price.** JLC's press release carries no price. Searched JLC press, aBlogtoWatch, Betteridge, The 1916 Company, Watches of Switzerland.
