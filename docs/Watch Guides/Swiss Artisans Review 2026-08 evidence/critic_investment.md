# Adversarial Critique — Investment / Collectability

**Critic:** investment & collectability (§3.3, hostile brief)
**Guide:** Swiss Artisans (JLC × VC), 2026 Edition, 21 cards
**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Evidence base:** the five venue files in this directory. Every financial claim
below cites a venue and date from those files. Anything not so grounded is
labelled **[my judgment]**.

**Standing caveats I am bound by.** WatchCharts, Chrono24, eBay and parts of the
auction pipeline were HTTP-403 blocked; most ask data is search-snippet grade.
Realized auction prices are the only high-quality evidence in the set. Asks are
never treated as comps. Where I convert JPY I use **¥145/USD as my own working
rate** — note that the source pages printed self-conversions implying ¥133
(Jackroad) and ¥149 (BEST ISHIDA), so every JDM dollar figure below carries
roughly ±10%.

## Verdict

This plan spends **$241,500 at band midpoints** across 17 targets, and it is
mispriced in the direction that costs money: eight of the seventeen bands sit
above the realized evidence, and the three largest line items — Mercator,
Duomètre, Chronomètre Royal, **$80,500 or 33% of the entire budget** — are the
three worst combinations of illiquidity, negative trend and unvalidated pricing
in the document. The guide's own hunt list is close to inversely correlated with
the financial evidence: it ranks the Mercator (four yellow-gold auction no-sales
across 2023–24) at 9.5 and leaves out the 3110V Triple Calendrier, the only entry
in all 21 with an appreciating trend (+11.8% y/y), a clean risk score (30/100)
and three tightly-clustered 2026 realized comps. The live P7 negotiation at
$12,000 is not a discount — it is at or above the central tendency of realized
white-gold 42005 prices, and the "$14,000–17,000" band the user is negotiating
against is an artefact of unsold asks. Most damningly, the guide contains no
concept of round-trip friction: buying premium-inclusive at a house and selling
back through one costs roughly **32% before the market moves at all**, which
means at least twelve of these entries would need a third of their value in
appreciation just to return the purchase price.

### The Bonhams-vs-Christie's spread — my read

$6,000 against CHF 13,860 is a 2.3× gap and the guide's band lives on the wrong
side of it. My answer, ranked by contribution:

1. **Venue and bidder depth — the dominant factor.** Bonhams' "Fine Watches"
   sales are high-volume, thin-catalogue affairs where vintage and neo-vintage
   complications routinely trade at trade money. The tell is not the hammer, it
   is the **specialist estimate: Bonhams valued it at $6,000–8,000 before the
   sale.** A major house's own specialist, with the watch in hand, priced a WG
   42005/000G at well under half the guide band. It then hammered at ~$4,800,
   *below even that low estimate.* That is two independent signals from the same
   lot, not one bad day.
2. **Market drift — real but secondary.** WatchCharts has this reference at
   **−7.1% y/y and −11.5% over five years while the VC index rose +7.5%** —
   18.9 percentage points of relative underperformance (evidence_watchcharts P7).
   That explains maybe a quarter of the gap between a 2022 Christie's result and
   a 2023 Bonhams one, not the whole of it.
3. **Condition — and it argues the *opposite* way.** No condition text exists on
   either lot. But the Bonhams lot had *better stated completeness* than almost
   anything else in the file (box, undated Certificate of Origin, manual, service
   booklet). **The cheap one was the complete one.** Anybody explaining the
   $6,000 away as "a beaten example" is arguing against the only completeness
   data available.
4. **Not "something else."** The one result comfortably inside the guide band —
   Phillips **HK$127,000 (~$16,250)**, 2024-03-19 — is a **pink gold,
   boutique-only, 150-piece limited edition, numbered 10, immaculate, full set
   with COSC certificate** (evidence_auctions P7). Anchoring the band on it is
   precisely the misread this review exists to catch.

**Conclusion:** the honest central tendency for a standard WG 42005/000G in the
open auction channel in 2026 is **$10,000–13,000**. The dealer channel adds
$1,500–3,000 of retail margin on top of that, which is where the $12,000 sits.

### Why $12,000 is not the bargain it looks like

- **Chrono24 shows 8 listings of this exact sub-reference**, with a floor ask of
  **$11,723 + shipping** and four of nine observed asks under $13,300
  (evidence_chrono24 P7). $12,000 is *inside the ask distribution*, not below it.
  Asks are ceilings; a purchase price inside the ask band is, definitionally, not
  a discount.
- **The cheapest live WG dealer ask found anywhere is $10,732** (I Play Watch HK,
  Jan 2026 — evidence_ebay P7).
- **The $16.4–17.0k eBay "level" is one watch.** Seller `shu590218nona` (JP)
  relisted the same 38mm WG silver 42005/000G five consecutive months
  May–Aug 2025 at $16,992 → $16,422, each listing surviving 1–19 days before
  removal (evidence_ebay P7). That is a **single unsold watch with a declining
  ask**, and the guide's $14,000–17,000 band appears to have been built on
  exactly this class of number.
- **The JDM channel is not cheap here, which is unusual and worth noting.** Six
  JDM asks run ¥1,880,000–2,780,000 (evidence_yahoojp P7) ≈ **$12,970–19,170** at
  ¥145. The cheapest is a complete set with box and papers. The normal
  "JDM runs 20–40% under Western" rule does **not** apply to this reference.
- **WatchCharts cannot compute days-on-market for it** — "not enough sales data"
  — and logged only **2 recorded sales in January 2026**, with a **risk score of
  69/100, High Risk** (evidence_watchcharts P7).

### The exit math nobody has run

If the user pays $12,000 and later consigns to a good house: realistic hammer
today, given the record above, is **$9,000–11,000**; net of a ~10–15% seller's
commission that is **$7,600–9,900**. Day-one round-trip loss:
**$2,100–4,400, or 18–37%.** And that assumes it sells — this reference no-saled
twice at Sotheby's inside three weeks in 2024. **[my judgment on the commission
rate; the hammer range is from the table above.]**

### What would justify $12,000 on THIS example

Nothing about the *reference* justifies it. Only the *specimen* can:

- Full original set including the Certificate of Origin **and** COSC paper.
- A VC or competent-independent service invoice inside ~5 years. The cal. 1206
  regulator/dual-time is not a watch to buy with a deferred service (see the
  service section — that alone is a $1,200–2,500 latent liability).
- Case sharpness: unpolished Malte tonneau flanks, crisp lug chamfers, legible
  hallmarks. A refinished case on a shaped VC is a permanent 20–30% impairment
  **[my judgment]**.
- Original hands — the guide itself flags this and it is correct.
- Domestic seller with return rights. Four of nine Chrono24 asks are Hong Kong
  (evidence_chrono24 P7), and the one confirmed US ask is $15,500 — a **$2,900
  jurisdiction premium, not a condition premium.**

### The negotiating leverage the user is not using

**Eight of this exact sub-reference are listed on Chrono24 right now.** This is
not a scarce hunt. The guide's framing — "if your negotiation lands, it becomes
one of the collection's defining oddballs" — manufactures urgency that the supply
data flatly contradicts. If this deal dies, another example surfaces within
weeks, and on a reference falling 7% a year, waiting is *paid*. Say so to the
seller.

## Illiquidity and exit risk — ranked

**The friction nobody has priced.** Buying premium-inclusive at a house and
selling back through one costs roughly **32% before the market moves**: you pay
hammer × 1.26, you receive hammer × ~0.85. Twelve of these seventeen entries
therefore need **a third of their value in appreciation just to return the
purchase price.** No band in the guide reflects this. **[my judgment on the ~15%
seller's commission; the 26% buyer's premium is established in evidence_auctions §1.]**

**1. P15 Aronde 81018 — the most illiquid object in the plan.**
**One priced Chrono24 listing worldwide** (2 total), and it asks **$25,605**

## The foundations — retail vs secondary

### P21 Master Control Q4168120 — pre-ordered at $17,000 = exactly full retail

**Confirmed:** US retail is $17,000 at The 1916 Company, Burdeens and
jaeger-lecoultre.com (evidence_chrono24 P21). Japanese AD retail is **¥2,992,000
incl. tax ≈ ¥2,720,000 ex-tax ≈ $18,760** at ¥145 (evidence_yahoojp P21) — so the
US list is the *cheapest global list*. **The user paid list: no discount, no
premium, no grey-market markup.** There is **no secondary market at all**:
Chrono24 shows Price-on-Request only, WatchCharts has no model page, eBay
returned nothing.

**Has the user overpaid? Against the market, no — they paid what everyone pays.
Against reality, yes, in the sense that every full-retail steel watch does.**

The two comparables in the evidence are unkind:
- **A Master Control Date Q4018420 in very good condition asks $7,100** on
  WatchCharts (evidence_watchcharts P21) — simpler reference, but it shows how
  steeply Master Controls settle off list.
- **The user's own P16** MUT Réserve de Marche asks **$5,037–6,000 pre-owned** and
  is running **−14.5% y/y** — this is the template, not an exception.

**Expect Q4168120 to settle at $10,000–12,500 pre-owned within 24–36 months, a
−30% to −41% mark-to-market [my judgment, anchored on the two comparables above].**
The chronometer certification and integrated bracelet may soften that; there is
no evidence either way, and absence of secondary supply is not evidence of
strength. **Book this as consumption, not as an asset**, and stop the guide from
implying that foundations carry investment logic.

### P20 Polaris Geographic Q9078640 — bought at $19,100 against ~$16,100 retail

**This one is a real, quantified error.** The user paid **$3,000 (+18.6%) over US
list** on a current-production, non-limited steel watch.

- Retail confirmed at **$16,100 excl. tax / €17,700 incl. VAT**
  (evidence_auctions P20, evidence_chrono24 P20).
- Secondary asks are **$18,000–19,171** on Chrono24 and **$14,200–18,450** across
  three simultaneous eBay listings in one month. The eBay collector's own read:
  *"a 30% ask spread across three simultaneous listings with zero verified sales
  is exactly what an unformed secondary market looks like."* Grey dealers list
  above retail purely on availability — this is **not appreciation**.
- **The one hard number in the whole file is the JDM buyback: ピアゾ, dated
  2025-08-02, ¥1,250,000 for an UNWORN Q9078640** (evidence_yahoojp P20). At ¥145
  that is **$8,620**; at ¥133, $9,400. A trade-in is a hard floor, not a market
  price — but a Japanese dealer paying ~$8,600–9,400 cash for an unworn example
  while US grey dealers ask $18,000+ tells you exactly where liquidation value
  sits. Sibling Polaris buybacks from the same dated table run ¥850,000–1,120,000,
  so the figure is internally consistent.

**Realistic mark-to-market today: $12,000–14,000 [my judgment, triangulating the
JDM buyback floor against grey asks and the ~$8,000 Polaris collection average on
WatchCharts]. Loss already incurred against the $19,100 paid: $5,000–7,000.**

There is no mechanism by which a 2024-launch, non-limited, steel Polaris in a new
colourway appreciates. **Do not compound it — this is a hold-and-wear.** The
lesson that should be written into the guide's methodology page: *pay list or
below on current-production steel; over-list is a fee for impatience and it is
never recovered.*

## Service-cost exposure — the complication tax

None of the 17 bands account for maintenance, and four entries carry a latent
liability large enough to change the buy decision. Current market rates:

- **VC time-only overhaul: $700–1,500. VC complicated (chronograph, perpetual
  calendar): $2,000–5,000.** ([Vacheron Constantin service pricing](https://watchmydiamonds.com/blog/post/how-much-does-the-vacheron-constantin-restoration-cost/), [WatchUSeek](https://www.watchuseek.com/threads/vacheron-constantin-servicing-costs.839778/))
- **JLC standard overhaul: ~$500–1,000; UK mechanical list from £710.**
  ([A to Z Watch Repair 2026 guide](https://atozwatchrepair.co.uk/jaeger-lecoultre-repair-servicing-the-expert-uk-guide-for-2026/))
- **JLC Duomètre specifically: quoted $2,200 USD; CAD $2,750 in 2025, up from
  CAD $2,200 in 2023.** Some Duomètres require service in Le Sentier; 9-week

### What this means for the plan

1. **P8 Master Grand Réveil is the worst ratio in the plan by a mile.** A
   perpetual calendar plus a mechanical alarm in a $12,000 steel watch means one
   service costs **25–42% of the purchase price**. The guide's own card says "buy
   only with strong service evidence; complexity makes deferred maintenance a
   false economy" — correct, and it should say so with a number. **A $11,359
   China-sourced example with no service history is not an $11,359 watch; it is a
   $15,000 watch with a hidden invoice** — and every Western ask is $17,800+
   (evidence_chrono24 P8), which is roughly the China price *plus the service*.
   That is the market telling you the true cost.
2. **P7's service liability is live in the negotiation.** $1,200–2,500 on a
   $12,000 purchase is 10–20%. If the seller cannot produce a service invoice,
   deduct it from the offer explicitly — that alone moves the fair price from
   $11,500 to ~$9,800.
3. **P11's is uninsurable.** A case-actuated chronograph is not a mechanism an
   independent will touch, and JLC parts availability for a 2006–2012 limited
   collaboration is a genuine question **[my judgment]**. Combine that with 40%
   auction sell-through and this entry is a liability, not an asset.
4. **The plan's total lifetime service burden.** Seventeen watches averaging
   ~$1,800 per service on a 6-year cycle is **~$5,100/year in maintenance at full
   build-out [my judgment]** — roughly 2.1% of the acquisition budget annually,
   forever. That number belongs on the methodology page.
