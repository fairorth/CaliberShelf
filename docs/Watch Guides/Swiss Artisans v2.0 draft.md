## The thesis

Modern fine watchmaking is a conversation between three places, and this
collection owns the conversation.

**Paris — the Idea.** Abraham-Louis Breguet, born Neuchâtel 1747, set up at
the Quai de l'Horloge in 1775 and proceeded to invent a startling share of
everything: the overcoil balance spring (1795) that beats inside a century of
fine calibres including JLC's and VC's; the pare-chute anti-shock (c.1790);
the self-winding perpétuelle; the souscription watch (1796); the tourbillon
(patented 1801); and the visual grammar — coined hands, secret signature,
guilloché, those numerals — that every dress watch on earth still speaks.
David Salomons said carrying a Breguet is like carrying a genius's brain in
your pocket. He was underselling it: the genius's brain is inside the *other*
watches too.

**The Vallée de Joux — the Engine.** LeCoultre, from 1833, turned ideas into
industrialized precision: the millionomètre, the great ébauches, calibre 920.
The valley engineered what Paris imagined.

**Geneva — the Cathedral.** Vacheron Constantin, from 1755 the oldest
watchmaker alive, composed engineering into objects with Geneva Seal
solemnity. VC's cal 1003 is JLC's 803 in Geneva dress; VC's cal 1120 is
JLC's 920 — the engine that powered the Royal Oak, the Nautilus and the 222,
and that JLC never once signed.

**And the punchline that closes the loop:** in the 1990s, Breguet acquired
Lemania of L'Orient — and the house of ideas physically moved *into the
Engine's valley*, where it builds today. Idea, Engine, Cathedral: three
roles, one geography by the end, one collection.

**The collection rule (unchanged, now with three houses to discipline):** a
watch earns a chapter only if it is **(a) a documented crossing between the
houses**, or **(b) the definitive statement of a virtue one house owns
outright**. Beautiful is not sufficient. Prestigious is not sufficient. If
the card cannot say what the watch *teaches*, the watch is padding.

**The hidden triangle** (tell this one at dinner): the chronometry covenant,
renewed roughly once a century, once per house — Breguet named **Horloger de
la Marine Royale, 1815** · VC trademarks **Chronomètre Royal, 1907** · JLC's
**Geophysic True Second, 2015**. Three houses making the same promise:
telling the truth about time.

### Ch. 1 · 1783 · The grammar of watchmaking
**Breguet Classique — refs verified Aug 2026: 7147 silvered guilloché

**What it teaches:** open any dress-watch catalog from any brand and you are

### Ch. 2 · 1796 → 2015 · The idea, made visible ★ THE CORNERSTONE (owned)
**Breguet Tradition 7097 "Automatique Seconde Rétrograde" · cal 505SR · 40mm
· OWNED — the collection's most beloved piece, and now its opening chapter**

**What it teaches:** in 1796 Breguet designed the souscription watch — one
hand, one barrel dead-center, symmetric bridges, sold by subscription to
democratize genius. In 2005 the Tradition line turned that 200-year-old
architecture *face-out*: what was hidden becomes the dial. Your 7097 shows
the pare-chute doing its work at 10 o'clock, the retrograde seconds arcing
where a souscription's hand once swept, the whole movement performing its own
history in real time. It is the single clearest possible statement of this
collection's thesis — **the Idea, made visible** — and it was the cornerstone
before the guide knew to look for it. *(Fact-check, Aug 2026: the calibre — officially 505SR1 — is NOT a borrowed
ultra-thin; it is a Tradition-family movement built on the souscription
pocket-watch architecture (507DR lineage) with a hammer rotor quoting
Breguet's perpétuelle. The movement isn't just displaying the 1796 idea —
it IS the 1796 idea, re-engineered. Refs for the record: 7097BB/G1/9WU
white gold · 7097BR/G1/9WU rose gold.)*

**Curatorial note:** this watch anchoring the collection re-ranks everything:
the guide is no longer "JLC × VC with a guest" — it is a three-voice fugue
and this is the subject stated first.

> **OWNED** · no band needed · the seriousness, already in the case

### Ch. 3 · 1815 → 1954 · In the service of the state
**Breguet Type XX Aéronavale — neo-vintage ref 3800 (3800ST/92/9W6, steel/
black — the value path) or a 1950s–60s Type 20 (the romance path)**

**What it teaches:** the house of ideas was also a house of *duty*. In 1815
Breguet became Horloger de la Marine Royale — precision as a naval
instrument, the covenant's first signing. From 1954 the same house was
delivering Type 20 flyback chronographs to the French military — the Air
Force first (1955–59), the Aéronavale by 1960: the inventor's firm building
military tools, guilloché nowhere in sight. This chapter keeps
the collection honest — genius that reports for work. It also gives the
Breguet voice its sport slot, beside the Polaris and the Overseas.

**Alternate teller:** a Marine (neo-vintage 3400-family, or the current 5517)
tells the 1815 half without the aviation romance — one slot, your choice of
century.

**Variant & buying wisdom:** for the 3800 — honest dial, working flyback,
no polish craters; the neo-vintage run is the affordable door into a lineage
whose vintage examples have already been discovered.

**The sharp play (verified Aug 2026):** the reference to hunt is the
**Aéronavale 3800 — no date** (1995): matt printed dial, typically serifed
numerals (both serif variants exist across the run — not an authenticity
tell), white-lacquer hands — the closest civilian echo of the 1950s military
Type 20, which carried no date because the military didn't order one. The date-at-6
examples are the **Transatlantique 3820** (1998): glossy dial, applied
polished indexes, date inside the 6 o'clock counter — a dressier civilian
cousin, acceptable only at a clear discount to a comparable 3800; for this
chapter the no-date is the object. **Skip for this slot:** Type XXI 3810
(2004, 42mm, central-minutes — a later, different story) and Type XXII 3880
(2010, 10 Hz — a tech flex, not this chapter). **Inspection:** working
flyback (reset on the fly while running), bezel intact and clicking,
unpolished case edges, and ask when the cal 582 (Lemania-based) was last
serviced — budget ~$1k if never. Box/papers are nice-not-necessary at these
prices.

**Status — OFFERS OUT (2026-08-14):** non-binding offers at $4,250 submitted
across a dozen-plus Chrono24 listings — fishing for the motivated seller who
knows the December solds ($3,751–4,939). Before accepting any bite: confirm
the exact ref (some cheap listings are date-at-6 Transatlantique 3820s, not
3800s) and tritium vs luminova dial; total landed cost in writing; flyback
reset-while-running on video; service history.

**Owner-observed market (Chrono24, 2026-08-13):** ~20 listings under $5,500,
sharpest example $4,500 (no box/papers) — a far lower ask floor than this
draft's first guess, and with supply that deep, clearing prices likely sit
lower still. **One honest caveat on the "75% off retail" framing:** the
~$27,200 new watch is the 2023-generation Type 20/XX — 42mm, in-house
flyback, a different watch — so the 3800 isn't that watch discounted; it's
the 39mm Lemania-based ancestor the market hasn't bothered to love yet.
Deep-supply-and-cheap also means weak demand: for a story-driven buyer who
isn't purchasing an exit, that is precisely the good kind of unloved.

> **Band (validated 2026-08-14): $3,800–5,200 (ref 3800, no box/papers)** ·
> med conf (~6 solds: Bonhams HK ~$3,610 Aug-24 & $4,939 Dec-25,

### Ch. 7 · 1951 · The automatic, argued differently
**Jaeger-LeCoultre Futurematic E501 · cal 497 · ~37mm · 1951–1957**

**What it teaches:** when the industry agreed the automatic should simply add
a rotor and keep the crown, JLC disagreed in public: crown deleted, set from
a caseback disc, power-reserve indicator standing guard. The boldest
production statement of the automatic decade — not "we can too" but "you're
all doing it wrong." Your 2026 Master Control (F4) quotes its twin-register
dial deliberately: this watch is why that watch looks the way it does.

**Variant & buying wisdom:** steel, silver/beige dial, and the back-set must
work — parts are unobtanium; a broken setting disc is a paperweight with
provenance. Originality outranks cosmetic perfection.

> **Band:** $4,000–6,500 · low conf · steel, honest dial, working back-set ·
> **VALUE — best story-per-dollar in the collection** · Buy first.

### Ch. 8 · 1956–1960s · The watch that speaks
**Jaeger-LeCoultre Memovox Automatic Calendar E855 · cal 825 (bumper) · 37mm
· 1960–c.1974**

**What it teaches:** the alarm is JLC's signature voice — literally. The 1956
Memovox was the world's first automatic alarm wristwatch; the E855 adds the
date and keeps the period charm that the "automatic" is a bumper you can
feel thump. Set it at dinner, hand it to a friend, watch their face when it
rings.

**Variant & buying wisdom:** steel, silver dial, applied markers; pay for a
clean alarm disc and a strong case. Auction channel runs $1,700–2,500 for
trade-money lots — the discount is real and so is the reason.

> **Band:** $3,000–4,250 · med conf · steel, serviced, dealer channel ·
> **VALUE** · Buy second.

### Ch. 10 · 1967–1977 · The engine JLC never signed
**The calibre 920/1120 story — told by the VC 222 (Canon) and its buyable
descendant, the gen-1 Overseas (line from 1996; target ref 42042)**

**What it teaches:** JLC's 1967 calibre 920 — full-rotor, 2.45mm — was so
good that AP, Patek and Vacheron all adopted it, and JLC never once used it
under its own name. As VC's 1120 it powered the 1977 222, Jörg Hysek's
integrated-bracelet answer to the Royal Oak and Nautilus — which both ALSO
ran on the 920. Every icon your friends are waitlisted for shares one
engine; the engine came from the valley. This is the chapter you teach from.

**How to own it:** the honest object is the 222 (steel 44018) — Canon money
since the 2022 reissue, standing order rules apply. The buyable descendant
is the gen-1 Overseas, caveat printed honestly (fact-checked Aug 2026): the
1996 debut ref was the **42040** on cal 1310; the slightly later **42042**
runs the upgraded cal **1311** — both Girard-Perregaux-based, so either
carries the lineage, not the calibre. A fairly-priced 1120-powered VC
outranks both for this chapter *(candidate refs TBV)*.

**Variant & buying wisdom (42042):** steel, bracelet sharp and unstretched —
the bracelet is the design. Full set strongly preferred.

> **Band (42042):** $6,500–12,500 · med conf (4 solds ~12mo) · steel,
> dealer/auction · CORE · *222: Canon, standing order, band TBV*

### Ch. 11 · 1972 · Geneva breaks symmetry
**Vacheron Constantin "1972" asymmetric — original 1970s ref 35202-family, or

**What it teaches:** in the year of the Royal Oak, when the industry bet

**Variant & buying wisdom:** condition-first shaped-case hunt either way —

> **Band (37010 echo):** $8,500–12,000 · med conf · sharp unpolished case,

### Ch. 12 · 1970–1987 · The quartz winter: keepers of the flame
**Chaumet-era Breguet — a Daniel Roth-hand revival piece. Candidate refs
